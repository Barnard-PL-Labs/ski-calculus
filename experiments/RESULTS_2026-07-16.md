## Headline: the M4 crossover is now measured, not projected

Apple M4 full chip (10 core, all-core, measured previously): 2,416,488 /s.
**K=40 measures 2,479,467 /s — +2.6% over the M4's whole chip**, on one ~$40,
~3 W ECP5-85. The draft predicted this crossover at ~38 cores; the fit now puts
it at 39 and K=40 demonstrates it directly.

## Discrepancy vs the draft's current numbers

Draft (pre-split firmware, K=4/8/16): 241,670 / 512,152 / 1,021,698
-> slope 63,723/core, projection 3.82 M/s.

New slope is **-4.7%**. The design is unchanged (same commit that carried those
numbers) and the measurement loop is identical, so the difference is the
firmware split. Unexplained -- the GA cores are autonomous and the CDC sits
only on the register interface, which the inner loop never touches. Note the
old K=4 point was itself 60,417/core (~6% internal scatter across the old three
points); the new five sit inside that spread and fit far better (R^2 0.9996 vs
a visibly noisier old line). Decide before submission: re-fit on the new
self-consistent set (recommended), or reconcile the 5%.

## K=45 / K=50: not measurable on the current stack

Both fail with `std::bad_alloc` in **yosys**, before nextpnr starts, under the
Fargate image's `BUILD_MAX_MEMORY_MB=12288`. Not the routing-congestion cliff
theorised in July -- that theory came from the old droplet path, where yosys
completed and nextpnr was merely slow. Filed: Manhattan-Reasoning-Cloud#79.
Ceiling is between K=40 (builds) and K=45 (fails).
